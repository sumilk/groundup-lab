# LA02 video — test passage (step 1 of the [voice plan](LA02_VIDEO_VOICE_PLAN.md))

**Purpose:** the text used for the delivery test (step 2) and the narration for the two-minute audiovisual sample (step 3). **Source:** LA02 §1, "one system, two pictures". **Assumed viewer:** knows LA01 and school algebra; nothing else.

**Status:** approved 2026-11-01. **Gate for this step:** read it aloud at a teaching pace. Anything that sounds like reading rather than explaining gets rewritten before step 2.

**Length:** about 225 spoken words plus 6.5 s of authored pauses, so roughly 1 min 30 s.

---

**VISUAL:** Left half only. A grid titled **input: the amounts (x, y)**. The two equations are written above it.

> Here are two equations of the kind you've solved in school. Two x minus y equals one. And x plus y equals five.

**VISUAL:** Each line draws as its equation is spoken. Each equation's text is tinted to match its line.

> On its own, each equation is a line: every point on it makes that equation true. A point on both lines makes both true. So the solution is wherever they cross.

**⏸ 2 — read the crossing point off the grid.**

**VISUAL:** A dot at (2, 3), with the label "x = 2, y = 3".

> Two across, three up. x is two, and y is three.

**VISUAL:** The coefficients in the equations light up column by column: 2 above 1, then −1 above 1, then the right-hand side 1 above 5. They lift out of the equations into a grid labelled **A**. The right half now opens: a second grid titled **output: where the amounts land**, with the blue arrow (2, 1), the teal arrow (−1, 1) and a green target at (1, 5).

> Read the same equations down the columns instead of across the rows. The numbers beside x are two and one. The numbers beside y are minus one and one. Each column is an arrow: blue goes two right and one up, teal goes one left and one up. The numbers on the right, one and five, are a target.

> Put the columns side by side and you have the matrix A. And the equations are asking the question from chapter one: how much blue, and how much teal, reaches the target?

**VISUAL:** The dot (2, 3) on the left pulses once. Nothing moves on the right yet.

> We've already found an answer on the left: two and three. So take two blue arrows, then three teal ones. Where do you think you'll end up?

**⏸ 3 — predict where two blue and three teal will land.**

**VISUAL:** On the right, two blue arrows lay tip to tail to (4, 2), then three teal arrows to (1, 5). The calculation builds under the panel as each part is spoken:
`2·(2, 1) + 3·(−1, 1) = (4 − 3, 2 + 3) = (1, 5)`

> Two blues: four right, two up. Three teals: three left, three up. Across, four minus three is one. Up, two plus three is five. One, five: exactly the target.

**⏸ 1.5 — read the finished calculation.**

**VISUAL:** An arrow labelled **A** draws from the left panel to the right, carrying the dot (2, 3) to the target (1, 5).

> So the same pair, two and three, has two jobs. On the left it's a place: where the lines cross. On the right it's a recipe: the amounts that reach the target.

> With two unknowns, you can draw the lines. With seven hundred and sixty-eight unknowns, you can't draw anything. But you can still ask whether these columns can reach the target.

## Notes for review

- **Checklist coverage:**
  - *Question:* how much blue and teal reaches the target.
  - *Prediction:* where two blue and three teal will land. This is a real prediction for a newcomer, because the answer is the insight itself.
  - *Worked example:* entry-by-entry arithmetic.
  - *Explanation:* the same pair is both a place and a recipe.
  - *What it changes:* the 768-unknown line.

  Following the plan, it opens with a familiar system rather than a question.
- **Delivery test cases:**
  - *Motivation:* the opening and the last paragraph.
  - *Equation in context:* "Two blues… exactly the target".
  - *Thinking pause:* ⏸ 3.
- **Separate spaces:** the amounts (2, 3) appear only on the left and the target (1, 5) only on the right. They are linked only through A.
- **Maths checked:** 2·2 − 3 = 1 and 2 + 3 = 5. Also 2·(2, 1) + 3·(−1, 1) = (4 − 3, 2 + 3) = (1, 5). The blue path ends at (4, 2) and the teal path at (1, 5), so both fit a grid running x −1…5, y 0…6.
- **Decided at review (2026-11-01):** the A arrow carries the "recipe → landing place" link with no narration (the sentence "Multiplying by A turns the recipe into the place it lands" was dropped). The passage now ends on the question, without a line about where the chapter goes next. The passage text is approved for step 2.
